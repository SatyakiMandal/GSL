What this report is, and is not

This is a structured case study, not a statistically validated causal finding, and not investment advice. It identifies days where notable news coverage coincided with an unusual company-specific price move. Coincidence in time is not evidence that an article caused a price move.

  • This is a structured case study, not a statistically validated causal finding. One company over one date range yields too few distinct incidents for the sentiment/abnormal-return relationship to carry statistical significance; a proper event study spans dozens of companies and events.
  • Flagged days are days on which unusual coverage COINCIDED WITH an unusual benchmark-adjusted return. Coincidence in time is not evidence that an article caused a price move.
  • 2 flagged day(s) show a price move in the opposite direction to the coverage's tone. That is not necessarily an error: news can be already priced in, or the day's move driven by something the coverage did not capture.

Summary

This report examines Yes Bank (YESBANK.NS) between 01 January 2025 and 01 January 2026, covering 250 trading days. It collects what the Indian financial press published about the company over that period, measures how the stock moved relative to ^NSEI, and identifies days where notable coverage and an unusual company-specific price move happened to fall together.

Price moves are reported as abnormal returns: the company's actual return minus what would have been expected given the benchmark's move that day, using a market model fitted on price history from before this window. This separates the company's own move from the market's, so a day when everything fell is not mistaken for company-specific news.

Across 300 collected items of coverage, 6 candidate days met both tests. The most prominent was 03 June 2025, with an abnormal return of -9.49% alongside negative coverage.

Of the 6 flagged days, 4 showed a price move in the direction the coverage's tone would suggest and 2 did not. Days where the two disagree are kept in the report rather than filtered out, because they are often the more interesting cases.

Method and provenance

Abnormal return. fitted on 120 trading days before 2025-01-01. Prices came from yfinance (company) and yfinance (benchmark). Abnormal returns are standardised against the estimation-window residual SD.

Timestamp alignment. An item published after the 15:30 IST close is attributed to the next trading day, since it could not have moved that day's close. 102 of the collected items fell after the close.

Sentiment. Scored with FinBERT, a finance-tuned model, rather than a general-purpose sentiment library — ordinary financial phrasing such as "beat expectations but missed guidance" is read incorrectly by generic tools. This score is what drives incident detection and the abnormal-return-direction check above.

Emotion. A secondary tag from GoEmotions (Demszky et al., 2020), a 27-emotion model trained on Reddit comments — not a finance-tuned model, and reading formal financial-press prose is a genuine domain mismatch. It is included as texture (fear vs. anger vs. disapproval alongside a shared "negative" FinBERT score can distinguish, say, a regulatory probe from a hostile FPO withdrawal) and never affects relevance, incident flagging, or the direction check. Scored on the headline only, and left blank below a 30% confidence threshold rather than forced to a low-confidence guess.
