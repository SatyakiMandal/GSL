Summary

This report examines Tata Motors (TMCV.NS) between 01 January 2025 and 01 January 2026, covering 36 trading days. It collects what the Indian financial press published about the company over that period, measures how the stock moved relative to ^NSEI, and identifies days where notable coverage and an unusual company-specific price move happened to fall together.

Price moves are reported as abnormal returns: the company's actual return minus the benchmark's return that day. There was not enough price history before this window to fit a full market model, so the stock is assumed to move one-for-one with the index. For a stock that is more volatile than the market, this assumption will overstate the abnormal return.

That is a legitimate result: it suggests the period contained no single dominant, market-moving story about this company. It can also mean the thresholds are set too strictly, or the window is too short for the coverage baseline to mean much.

Method and provenance

Abnormal return. only 0 estimation observations before 2025-01-01 (need 40); using market-adjusted returns with beta fixed at 1.0. Prices came from yfinance (company) and yfinance (benchmark). Abnormal returns are standardised against the analysis-window SD (weaker: inflated by the events in it).

Timestamp alignment. An item published after the 15:30 IST close is attributed to the next trading day, since it could not have moved that day's close. 1 of the collected items fell after the close.

Sentiment. Scored with FinBERT, a finance-tuned model, rather than a general-purpose sentiment library — ordinary financial phrasing such as "beat expectations but missed guidance" is read incorrectly by generic tools. This score is what drives incident detection and the abnormal-return-direction check above.

Emotion. A secondary tag from GoEmotions (Demszky et al., 2020), a 27-emotion model trained on Reddit comments — not a finance-tuned model, and reading formal financial-press prose is a genuine domain mismatch. It is included as texture (fear vs. anger vs. disapproval alongside a shared "negative" FinBERT score can distinguish, say, a regulatory probe from a hostile FPO withdrawal) and never affects relevance, incident flagging, or the direction check. Scored on the headline only, and left blank below a 30% confidence threshold rather than forced to a low-confidence guess.
