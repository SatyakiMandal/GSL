What this report is, and is not

This is a structured case study, not a statistically validated causal finding, and not investment advice. It identifies days where notable news coverage coincided with an unusual company-specific price move. Coincidence in time is not evidence that an article caused a price move.

Summary

This report examines IndusInd Bank (INDUSINDBK.NS) between 01 March 2025 and 31 March 2025, covering 19 trading days. It collects what the Indian financial press published about the company over that period, measures how the stock moved relative to ^NSEI, and identifies days where notable coverage and an unusual company-specific price move happened to fall together.

Price moves are reported as abnormal returns: the company's actual return minus what would have been expected given the benchmark's move that day, using a market model fitted on price history from before this window. This separates the company's own move from the market's, so a day when everything fell is not mistaken for company-specific news.

Across 257 collected items of coverage, 3 candidate days met both tests. The most prominent was 11 March 2025, with an abnormal return of -27.20% alongside strongly negative coverage.

Of the 3 flagged days, 2 showed a price move in the direction the coverage's tone would suggest and 1 did not. Days where the two disagree are kept in the report rather than filtered out, because they are often the more interesting cases.

Method and provenance

Abnormal return. fitted on 120 trading days before 2025-03-01. Prices came from yfinance (company) and yfinance (benchmark). Abnormal returns are standardised against the estimation-window residual SD.

CAR significance. The t column next to CAR assumes independent, normally distributed abnormal returns over a large sample — an assumption a single company's own handful of trading days does not meet, so it is printed only as the conventional figure, not a validated one. The p column is a permutation-test alternative that does not need that assumption: many random same-length windows are drawn from this company's own abnormal-return series (excluding every other flagged day, so the null is not contaminated by real events), and p is the fraction of those placebo CARs at least as extreme as the real one. It is still only valid for this one run on this one company — it says nothing about whether the pattern would replicate elsewhere — but it does not inherit the independence assumption t does.

Timestamp alignment. An item published after the 15:30 IST close is attributed to the next trading day, since it could not have moved that day's close. 80 of the collected items fell after the close.

Sentiment. Scored with FinBERT, a finance-tuned model, rather than a general-purpose sentiment library — ordinary financial phrasing such as "beat expectations but missed guidance" is read incorrectly by generic tools. This score is what drives incident detection and the abnormal-return-direction check above.

Emotion. A secondary tag from GoEmotions (Demszky et al., 2020), a 27-emotion model trained on Reddit comments — not a finance-tuned model, and reading formal financial-press prose is a genuine domain mismatch. It is included as texture (fear vs. anger vs. disapproval alongside a shared "negative" FinBERT score can distinguish, say, a regulatory probe from a hostile FPO withdrawal) and never affects relevance, incident flagging, or the direction check. Scored on the headline only, and left blank below a 30% confidence threshold rather than forced to a low-confidence guess.

Sentiment/return correlation. Pearson r over 14 news-carrying day(s) in this run; descriptive only, not a significance test, and not comparable across runs with different day counts. A Pearson correlation across a handful of trading days is descriptive, not a significance test — treat it as a single additional lens on the same daily table above, not as proof that sentiment predicts price.

Emotion valence vs return. 8 day(s) had a confident dominant emotion (GoEmotions, headline-only, secondary to FinBERT); grouped by the paper's own positive/negative/ambiguous clustering. Descriptive only — small day counts per group, and this never affects incident flagging or ranking.
